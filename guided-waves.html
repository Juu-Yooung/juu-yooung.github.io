PROBLEM

Segmented excitation leaves a spatial signature.

Conventional omnidirectional SH-wave transducers approximate a circular source using discrete elements. Amplitude and phase variation can persist in the near field, complicating predictable inspection of tightly spaced features.

○

SOLUTION

Transfer the deformation through a cylindrical waveguide.

The waveguide develops an axisymmetric torsional field. Its continuous annular contact transfers that field into radially propagating SH waves, preserving circumferential uniformity at the source.

MY CONTRIBUTION

NDT application: nuclear fuel-rod inspection.

  1. Conceive the application

    Define guided-wave inspection for the geometry of nuclear fuel rods.

  2. Design the source geometry

    Design a conformal 3D-printed ring for axisymmetric actuation.

  3. Simulate the wavefield

    Compare disk and ring sources with finite-element wave analysis.

  4. Verify experimentally

    Test mock rods with and without an artificial fouling-type defect.

×

PROBLEM

A flat source does not match a cylindrical inspection surface.

A conventional disk transducer produces localized contact and asymmetric excitation. Unwanted circumferential components overlap the desired axial response, reducing the clarity of a defect-related signal.

○

SOLUTION

3D-print the active element to conform to the structure.

A 3D-printed piezoelectric ring couples around the rod and generates an axisymmetric axial wavefield. By suppressing unwanted circumferential modes, the conformal geometry overcomes a measurement barrier: the defect-induced response obscured by disk-source interference becomes distinguishable in the tested mock nuclear fuel-rod configuration.

MEASURED RESPONSE · 100 kHz

Recover the defect-induced signal.

Pulse-echo measurements compared mock nuclear fuel rods with and without an artificial defect. The ring source separates the axial response from circumferential-wave interference, making the defect-induced attenuation clearly distinguishable.
